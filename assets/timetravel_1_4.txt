==============================================================================
THE CASE OF THE ENCRYPTED LEDGER AND THE MISSING MAGIC — A FORENSIC
INVESTIGATION INTO TIME-TRAVEL v1.4
==============================================================================
By John H. Watson, M.D.
Late of the Army Medical Department

It was one of those grey, obstinate evenings in which the old Core 2 Duo
seemed to breathe more heavily than usual. The mechanical disk clicked with
a slow, judicial rhythm, like a magistrate tapping a pen against a desk.
Sherlock Holmes sat motionless before the terminal, his face illuminated by
columns of hexadecimal output.

"Watson," he said at last, "the case of v1.4 is not a case of memory alone.
It is a case of trust. The engine learned two new arts: to deduplicate large
files, and to encrypt what it remembers. But every new art creates a new
crime."

"Good heavens, Holmes," I replied, hanging my coat by the door. "What crime
could possibly survive the battery of tests?"

"Three," said Holmes, raising three thin fingers. "First: corrupted ledgers
that hide behind a broken seal. Second: orphan blocks that linger in the
dark after their references have vanished. Third: plaintext whispers escaping
from an encrypted vault."

He turned the screen toward me.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
I. THE DEDUP POOL
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

"Observe," Holmes began, "files of at least one mebibyte are no longer
written whole into the delta stream. They are cut into sixty-four kibibyte
fragments. Each fragment is hashed with BLAKE2b-256 and stored once in the
content-addressed pool:"

    .timetravel/blocks/<2hex>/<remaining hex>

"The .ttd record stores only the ordered list of hashes, Watson. We call it
TT_EV_CREATE_DEDUP. Identical fragments are shared between files, versions,
and even histories. Restoration is an act of reassembly, not duplication."

"And if a block is missing?" I asked.

"Then restoration must fail gracefully," Holmes replied. "No phantom content.
No partial lies."

"And if a block is corrupted?"

"Then its hash betrays it. BLAKE2b does not forgive."

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
II. THE ENCRYPTED VAULT
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Holmes lit his pipe and continued.

"A repository may now be born under secrecy:"

    timetravel start /path/to/project --encrypt

"The user speaks a passphrase twice. The passphrase itself is never stored.
From it, through PBKDF2-HMAC-BLAKE2b with a random salt and one hundred
thousand iterations, a thirty-two byte key is derived. The salt, iteration
count, and key verifier are sealed inside:"

    .timetravel/crypto.meta

"The payloads of records and the blocks of the dedup pool are then protected
by XChaCha20-Poly1305. Authenticated encryption, Watson. Not mere privacy,
but integrity."

"Then the store reveals nothing?" I asked.

Holmes shook his head.

"Not so fast. The content is hidden, but the skeleton remains visible.
Record headers, paths, timestamps, and file sizes are authenticated but not
encrypted. A historian needs names and dates; otherwise he cannot restore
anything. Therefore, v1.4 encryption protects content, but does not promise
full metadata secrecy."

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
III. THE MISSING MAGIC
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Holmes leaned forward.

"Now we arrive at the most instructive failure. The verify command was asked
to certify the health of a store. But a corrupted .ttd file may carry a
broken seal — an invalid TTDLTA01 magic. The store reader, designed to skip
unreadable archives, passed over it in silence. And verify, seeing no
records, declared the store clean."

"A false acquittal," I murmured.

"Precisely. A forensic examiner must inspect the seals before trusting the
absence of testimony. Therefore verify was hardened. It now walks every .ttd
file before the reader is allowed to speak. If the magic is absent, corrupt,
or too short, the file is denounced as corrupt and verification fails."

He showed me the order of interrogation:

    1. Validate TTDLTA01 magic on all .ttd files.
    2. Read and sort all records.
    3. Group records by path.
    4. For CREATE: payload size must match file size.
    5. For CREATE_DEDUP: every block must exist, match its expected size,
       and reproduce its BLAKE2b-256 reference.
    6. For MODIFY: the xdelta3 delta must decode against the reconstructed
       previous state.
    7. For DELETE: the reconstruction state is cleared.
    8. For encrypted repositories: AEAD authentication is enforced.

"And the verdict?" I asked.

"Zero for a clean store. Non-zero for corruption, tampering, missing blocks,
invalid magic, or authentication failure."

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
IV. THE FAIL-CLOSED GARBAGE COLLECTOR
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

"The dedup pool," Holmes continued, "requires discipline. Compact no longer
merely collapses delta chains. It now rewrites the store atomically and
performs garbage collection upon the blocks."

"And orphans?"

"Removed. Blocks referenced by no record are swept away. Non-hexadecimal
garbage inside the pool is also expelled. But observe the law of fail-closed
design, Watson: if compact cannot read or authenticate the history of an
encrypted repository, the garbage collector does not run. A wrong passphrase
must never become a weapon of deletion."

"A prudent doctrine," I said.

"The only doctrine," Holmes replied.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
V. THE AUDIT CHAMBERS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Holmes opened another ledger.

"The engine was examined under several unforgiving lights. AddressSanitizer
and UndefinedBehaviorSanitizer hunted memory corruption and undefined
behavior. ThreadSanitizer hunted races between threads. Valgrind hunted
leaks."

"And the result?"

"The battery was expanded. One hundred and four assertions across forty-one
sections: ninety-six functional assertions and eight dedicated Valgrind
memory-audit assertions. Under ThreadSanitizer, the Valgrind chamber is
sealed, for TSan and Valgrind cannot occupy the same bench. In that mode,
ninety-six assertions are judged and the Valgrind audit is skipped."

"And Valgrind itself?"

Holmes allowed himself the faintest smile.

"Zero bytes definitely lost. Zero bytes indirectly lost. Zero errors. The
metal confesses nothing."

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
VI. THE FINAL ENTRY
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The rain struck the window. The disk clicked once more and fell quiet.

"Then the case is closed," I said.

"It is," Holmes answered. "v1.4 learned to store less by understanding
repetition, and to protect more by demanding authentication. But remember,
Watson: encryption without integrity is merely decoration, deduplication
without verification is merely faith, and a test battery without failure
paths is merely theatre."

He closed the terminal.

"Let the ledger record that v1.4 did not merely grow features. It grew
suspicion."
