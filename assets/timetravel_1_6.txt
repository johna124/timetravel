==============================================================================
THE CASE OF THE SILENT WATCHER AND THE EXTENDED CHRONICLE — A FORENSIC
INVESTIGATION INTO TIME-TRAVEL v1.6
==============================================================================
By John H. Watson, M.D.
Late of the Army Medical Department

The fog had lifted from London, revealing a city that had grown more complex
in its machinery but no less demanding in its precision. Sherlock Holmes sat
before his terminal, the glow of the cursor reflecting in his eyes like a
lighthouse in a storm. He held a single file in his hand — not a directory,
not a project, but a solitary configuration script.

"Watson," he said, breaking the silence, "we have long assumed that the
historian must watch the entire library to record the change of a single
book. We were wrong. v1.6 teaches us that one can watch the whisper without
hearing the shout."

He tapped three keys. The screen filled with a new kind of log — detailed,
semantic, and terrifyingly precise.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
I. THE SILENT WATCHER (FILE REPOS)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

"Consider the old way," Holmes began, pacing the room. "To track a single
file, `nginx.conf`, we had to watch the entire `/etc` directory. We captured
thousands of irrelevant changes, bloated the store, and diluted the signal
with noise. It was inefficient. It was... messy."

"And now?" I asked.

"Now, the daemon watches only the file. Not its parent. Not its siblings.
Just the file. It creates a store in the parent directory, yes, but the
logic is isolated. If `nginx.conf` changes, it is recorded. If `hosts`
changes next to it? Silence. The daemon does not see it. It is as if the
file exists in a vacuum."

He showed me the test results. Section 45 of the battery.
`assert_ok("45.B7 sibling file NOT captured")`.

"It is surgical, Watson. For the developer who needs to track a single
script, or the sysadmin who fears the drift of a config file, this is
liberation. No more `.git/init` for a single text file. Just pure,
unadulterated versioning."

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
II. THE EXTENDED CHRONICLE (EXTLOG)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Holmes turned to the second marvel. He typed:
`./build/timetravel extlog src/main.c --repo src`

The output was not the simple list of timestamps we were used to. It was a
narrative.

[000002] MODIFY size=1.2K ratio=0.85 elapsed=1h #quantum_flux_capacitor

"Do you see the depth?" he asked. "The classic `log` tells you *when*.
The `extlog` tells you *what* and *how much*. It shows the scope of the
change. The size of the file. The ratio of the delta. The time elapsed
since the previous edit. And finally, the semantic summary — the tags
extracted from the wound itself."

"It is like reading the surgeon's notes instead of just the admission date,"
I observed.

"Precisely. And it is additive. The old `log` remains for quick glances.
But when you need to understand the evolution of a module, `extlog` is the
only tool that matters. It does not replace history; it illuminates it."


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
III. THE CLEANLINESS OF TAGS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

"There is one final detail," Holmes added. "The autotags. In v1.5, they
were raw. In v1.6, they are refined. Garbage tokens are filtered. Binary
files produce no hashtags. Uppercase is folded to lowercase. The tags are
no longer just identifiers; they are insights."

He showed me a test case:
`assert_contains("50.1 uppercase token folded", "#galactic_router")`
`pass("50.4 binary file produces no hashtag autotags")`

"The system learns to ignore the noise. It focuses on the signal. It is
the difference between hearing a crowd and listening to a conversation."

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
IV. THE FINAL VERDICT
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The rain struck the window again. The mechanical disk clicked, satisfied.
Holmes closed the terminal.

"v1.6 is not a revolution. It is a refinement. It allows us to watch the
small things with the same intensity as the large. It allows us to read the
history with greater depth."

He picked up his pipe.

"Let the ledger record that v1.6 did not just add features. It added
precision. And in precision, there is peace."
